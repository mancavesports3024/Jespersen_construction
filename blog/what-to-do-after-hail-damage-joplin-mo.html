What to do after hail damage in Joplin MO

A practical checklist for homeowners after a hail storm—before ladders, sales pitches, or signatures.

Blog · Updated for homeowners in Jasper County and the Joplin metro.

1. Stay safe first

Hail often comes with lightning and slick ground. Wait until the storm passes before walking the property. Avoid climbing the roof yourself—steep pitches, wet shingles, and hidden soft spots from prior leaks are easy to underestimate. If you see active leaks inside, contain water with buckets and call a professional for temporary protection if needed.

2. Document the date and storm

Insurance carriers tie coverage to specific events. Save local weather reports or news references that confirm hail in your area on a given day. Take wide photos of your home’s exterior from the ground—roof lines, siding, gutters, and any visible debris. If it is safe, capture close-ups of dented metal fixtures (vents, flashing, gutter strips) because metal can show hail more clearly than shingles from street level.

3. Walk the ground, not just the curb

Look for granules washed into downspout exits, cracked window screens, or splatter marks on AC units. These do not prove roof damage by themselves, but they support a timeline that something significant hit your property. Note anything unusual for when you talk to an inspector.

4. Call a local roofing contractor for an inspection—not just a flyer at the door

After regional storms, out-of-town crews appear quickly. You want a roofing contractor in Joplin MO who documents what they see, explains whether damage is cosmetic or functional, and can outline repair versus replacement without rushing you to sign an assignment of benefits you do not understand. Jespersen Construction focuses on built-right work and clear insurance claim help—including AI-assisted estimate review when you already have a carrier scope.

5. Understand the insurance sequence

Typically you file a claim, the carrier assigns an adjuster, and you receive an estimate. Your contractor compares field conditions to that estimate and may request supplements for code-required work or missed line items. For a fuller walkthrough, read how insurance claims work for roof damage. No article replaces your policy language—read your declarations and ask your agent about deadlines.

6. Act before secondary damage

Damaged shingles can allow water in slowly—stains appear months later. If an inspection shows compromised waterproofing, plan repairs within the timelines your insurer gives. If you are weighing cost, see how much a new roof costs in Missouri for the factors that move price.
